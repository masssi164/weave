# Jurisdiction and legal-risk context

This note is source-backed context for provider and jurisdiction exposure decisions. It is not legal advice and does not claim that Weave solves, avoids, neutralizes, or guarantees outcomes under any legal regime.

## Why this exists

Organizations may need to evaluate provider location, control, ownership, subprocessors, contractual terms, government access regimes, exportability, migration paths, and operational evidence. Weave makes adapter/provider posture and evidence visible so admins and operators can choose the next safe action.

## Source anchors

- **18 U.S.C. § 2713** states that a provider's obligations to preserve, back up, or disclose covered records or information under the Stored Communications Act apply regardless of whether the communication, record, or other information is located within or outside the United States. Source: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title18-section2713
- **U.S. Department of Justice CLOUD Act resources** describe the CLOUD Act as clarifying provider disclosure obligations and enabling executive agreements for cross-border access requests. Source: https://www.justice.gov/dag/cloudact
- **EDPB/EDPS response on the CLOUD Act** discusses EU data-protection concerns and the need to assess conflicts, transfer tools, and safeguards. Source: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/letters/edpb-edps-response-libecivilibe-cloud-act_en

## Weave framing

- Specific laws such as the U.S. CLOUD Act are jurisdiction-risk examples, not Weave features.
- Weave can record adapter/provider posture, hosting/control model, jurisdiction/provider exposure, caveats, evidence, and migration/replacement paths.
- Weave cannot promise immunity from lawful access, compliance certification, guaranteed sovereignty, guaranteed confidentiality, or perfect migration.
- Stronger claims require provider-specific legal, contractual, technical, and operational evidence outside this note.

## Required documentation behavior

- Keep specific legal regimes out of the README except for a brief pointer that legal-risk context is sourced elsewhere.
- Put provider/source links and unknown markers in the adapter registry.
- Pair sovereignty statements with implementation state, caveats, and readiness evidence.
- Route approval/action authority through MCP/domain-tool actions, not legal posture language.
